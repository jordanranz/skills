# Resume Refinement

Create credible, role-aligned resumes while preserving the candidate's factual record and visual system.

## Refine a resume

1. Locate the base resume, existing variants, candidate context, and target job description. Treat designated source files as read-only.
2. Identify the role's highest-signal requirements and map each to verified evidence. Mark unsupported requirements as gaps rather than solving them with keywords.
3. Choose the smallest effective pass:
   - `No pass`: the base resume already communicates the fit.
   - `Light pass`: revise the headline, summary, skills ordering, and a few high-signal bullets.
   - `Focused pass`: reshape more content when the role requires a materially different narrative.
4. Lead with relevant professional experience. Use projects as supporting evidence, not a substitute.
5. Preserve strong source bullets when they already provide better evidence than a rewrite.
6. Preserve the layout unless the user requests a redesign. Remove weak content before shrinking readable type.
7. For DOCX or PDF output, invoke the available document skills and visually inspect every rendered page.
8. Deliver a short filename identifying the candidate, employer, and optionally the role family. Recommend PDF for upload unless the form requests another format.

## Evidence rules

- Distinguish professional production work, professional exploration, substantial personal projects, and experiments.
- Do not invent or inflate metrics, scope, technologies, dates, leadership duration, customers, adoption, or production status.
- Do not copy company-specific language merely to mirror a posting.
- Use `led`, `built`, and `owned` only when supported. Do not weaken verified ownership with repeated `helped`.
- Generalize confidential internal names or strategy when external safety is uncertain.
- Use `Present` for ongoing employment unless the candidate directs otherwise.

## Tailoring order

Prioritize the headline, summary, skills, recent-role bullets, older high-signal experience, then projects.
